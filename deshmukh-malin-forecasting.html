Abstract

Linear models are strong and inexpensive baselines for long-term time-series forecasting, but they are usually evaluated on complete input windows, whereas deployed sensor streams contain isolated dropouts and multi-hour outages. This paper examines how standard linear forecasters degrade when their inputs are incomplete and proposes MALin, a missingness-aware linear forecaster that adds three lightweight elements to a RevIN-linear model: instance normalisation computed from observed entries only, a linear branch driven by the missingness mask, and training on windows corrupted with random point and block missingness. MALin and seven baselines (naive repeat, ridge regression, DLinear with zero filling and with interpolation, NLinear, RLinear, and RLinear with missingness-augmented training) were evaluated on the ETTh1, ETTh2, and ETTm1 datasets at horizons of 96 to 720 steps, under point and block missingness at rates of 10–70%, with identical fixed test masks for all models. Interpolation made point missingness largely harmless, but 50% block missingness still increased the MSE of interpolated DLinear and RLinear by 30% and 33% on average. MALin reduced the error under 30–70% block missingness by 3.8% on average relative to RLinear and by up to 9.5% on ETTm1, without a systematic loss on complete inputs. Diebold–Mariano tests with Holm correction found MALin significantly better than RLinear in 24 of 48 block-missingness settings and significantly worse in none of the 108 settings tested. The gains were concentrated on ETTh1 and ETTm1, while MALin was slightly but not significantly worse on ETTh2. An ablation showed that missingness-augmented training and observed-only normalisation account for most of the improvement, whereas the mask branch helps only when gaps are zero-filled.
